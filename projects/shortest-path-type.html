shortest-path typeface

computational & parametric design · Basics of Creative Coding, 2025/26

An alphabet generated by an algorithm, built in Grasshopper for Rhino. The starting idea was a typeface that imitates the growth of mycelium: a network of threads that grows denser wherever there should be a letter.

My first attempts used branching and L-systems, but the results were too geometric and repetitive to feel organic. I then switched to a shortest-path approach. Each letter is turned into a surface, split into a left and a right half and randomly populated with points, and more points are scattered on either side of it. Python components then build a graph of connections between the points within a set radius and use Dijkstra's algorithm to find the shortest paths from the outer points through the letter.

Instead of soft mycelium, the letters came out sharp, almost as if they were built from needles – better suited to visuals for a night club than to a natural aesthetic. Some letters are more legible than others, which gives the alphabet a mysterious character while still letting you recognise whole words.
